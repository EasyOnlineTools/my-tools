What Is a Scientific Calculator?

A scientific calculator is an advanced calculator used to solve mathematical problems beyond ordinary addition, subtraction, multiplication and division. It includes functions for trigonometry, logarithms, exponents, roots, factorials and mathematical constants.

Students, teachers, engineers and people working with mathematics and science can use scientific calculators to solve equations, check calculations and explore mathematical relationships.

Scientific Calculator Functions Explained

The calculator includes several common mathematical operations. Here is what each function does and when you might use it.

sin, cos and tan Trigonometric functions used to calculate relationships between angles and sides of triangles.
sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹ and tan⁻¹ Inverse trigonometric functions used to find an angle from a trigonometric ratio.
log Calculates the common logarithm, which uses base 10.
ln Calculates the natural logarithm, which uses the mathematical constant e as its base.
√ and x² Finds the square root of a number or multiplies a number by itself.
x³ and xʸ Calculates cubes and powers, such as 3³ or 2⁵.
x! Calculates the factorial of a non-negative whole number, such as 5! = 120.
π and e Inserts mathematical constants pi and Euler's number into an expression.
1/x Calculates the reciprocal of a number. For example, the reciprocal of 4 is 0.25.
|x| Returns the absolute value, or non-negative distance from zero.

Degrees (DEG) vs Radians (RAD)

Trigonometric functions use angles. An angle can be measured in degrees or radians, so selecting the correct angle mode is important.

Degree mode

A full circle contains 360 degrees. In degree mode, sin(30) means the sine of 30 degrees, which equals 0.5.

Radian mode

A full circle contains 2π radians. In radian mode, sin(π/6) equals 0.5. Radians are commonly used in higher mathematics and calculus.

Conversion formula:
Degrees = Radians × 180 ÷ π
Radians = Degrees × π ÷ 180

Important Calculation Notes

  • Logarithms and natural logarithms require a positive number.
  • Square roots require a non-negative number in real-number calculations.
  • Division by zero and reciprocal of zero are undefined.
  • Factorials are supported for non-negative whole numbers up to 170.
  • Inverse sine and cosine accept values from -1 to 1.
  • Results use standard JavaScript floating-point arithmetic and may be rounded for display.
  • The percentage button treats a number followed by % as that number divided by 100. For example, 50% = 0.5.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a scientific calculator used for?

A scientific calculator is used for arithmetic, trigonometry, logarithms, powers, roots, factorials and other advanced mathematical calculations.

Is this online scientific calculator free?

Yes. This calculator is free to use in a web browser. You do not need to install software or create an account.

What is the difference between DEG and RAD?

DEG measures angles in degrees, while RAD measures angles in radians. Select the mode that matches the angle used in your mathematical problem.

How do I calculate a power such as 2 to the power of 5?

Enter 2, press the xʸ button, enter 5 and press equals. The result is 32.

How do I calculate a factorial?

Enter a non-negative whole number and press the x! button. For example, 5! = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120.

Why does my calculator show an error?

An error may appear if an expression is incomplete, you divide by zero, enter an invalid logarithm or square root, or use a value outside the supported range of a function.

Can I use this calculator on a mobile phone?

Yes. The calculator is designed to work on mobile phones, tablets, laptops and desktop computers.

Does this calculator support keyboard input?

Yes. You can enter numbers and mathematical operators using your keyboard. Press Enter to calculate, Backspace to delete and Escape to clear the calculator.
