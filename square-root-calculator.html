What Is a Square Root?

A square root is a number that produces the original number when multiplied by itself. In other words, it is the reverse operation of squaring a number.

√N = R

In this formula, N is the original number and R is its principal square root. The principal square root is the non-negative value.

R × R = N

For example, the square root of 144 is 12 because 12 multiplied by 12 equals 144.

√144 = 12   because   12 × 12 = 144

What Is a Perfect Square?

A perfect square is a number that can be written as a whole number multiplied by itself. For example, 36 is a perfect square because 6 × 6 = 36.

Square Roots of Decimal Numbers

Square roots are not always whole numbers. For example, the square root of 10 is approximately 3.16227766. This calculator supports decimal input as well as whole numbers.

Square Root of Zero

Zero has a square root of zero because zero multiplied by zero is still zero.

√0 = 0

Important Facts About Square Roots

Can a Negative Number Have a Real Square Root?

A negative number does not have a real square root. This is because the product of a real number multiplied by itself cannot be negative. For example, both 5 × 5 and (-5) × (-5) equal positive 25.

Why Are There Two Numbers Whose Squares Equal 25?

Both 5 and -5 produce 25 when squared. However, the square root symbol √ represents the principal, non-negative square root. Therefore, √25 = 5.

What Is the Difference Between Squaring and Finding a Square Root?

Squaring means multiplying a number by itself. Finding a square root is the reverse operation. For example, 7² = 49, while √49 = 7.
