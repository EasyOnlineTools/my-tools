What Is Simple Interest?

Simple interest is interest calculated only on the original principal amount. It is commonly used for certain loans, investments and short-term financial calculations.

Simple Interest Formula

Simple Interest = (Principal × Rate × Time) ÷ 100

In this formula, the interest rate is the annual percentage rate and the time must be expressed in years.

Total Amount Formula

Total Amount = Principal + Simple Interest

Example of Simple Interest

Suppose you invest $10,000 at an annual interest rate of 5% for 3 years.

Simple Interest = (10,000 × 5 × 3) ÷ 100
Simple Interest = $1,500

Total Amount = $10,000 + $1,500
Total Amount = $11,500

How to Use This Simple Interest Calculator

Calculating Simple Interest for Months or Days

When you select months, the calculator converts the entered number of months into years by dividing by 12. When you select days, it converts the entered number of days into years using 365 days per year.

Time in Years = Months ÷ 12

Time in Years = Days ÷ 365

Simple Interest Calculator FAQs

What is simple interest?

Simple interest is interest calculated only on the original principal amount. Previously earned interest is not added to the principal when calculating future simple interest.

What information is needed to calculate simple interest?

You generally need the principal amount, annual interest rate and time period.

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is calculated on the original principal, while compound interest can be calculated on the principal plus accumulated interest.

Can this calculator calculate the total amount?

Yes. It calculates both the simple interest and the total amount after adding the interest to the original principal.

Can I calculate interest for months or days?

Yes. Select Months or Days from the Time Unit menu. The calculator converts the entered period into years before applying the simple interest formula.

Does this calculator use compound interest?

No. This calculator uses the simple interest formula and does not add previously earned interest to the principal.
