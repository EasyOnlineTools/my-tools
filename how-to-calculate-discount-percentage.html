How to Calculate Discount Percentage: Formula, Examples & Easy Guide

Discounts are common when shopping online and in stores. Knowing how to calculate discount percentage can help you understand how much money you are saving and what the final price of an item will be.

In this guide, you will learn how to calculate the discount percentage, discount amount, final sale price and original price using simple formulas and step-by-step examples.

What Is a Discount Percentage?

A discount percentage shows how much the original price has been reduced, expressed as a percentage of the original price.

For example, if a $100 product is offered at 20% off, the discount is $20 and the customer pays $80.

Key idea: A 20% discount means you save 20% of the original price and pay the remaining 80%.

Discount Percentage Formula

When you know the original price and sale price, you can calculate the discount percentage using this formula:

Discount Percentage = ((Original Price − Sale Price) ÷ Original Price) × 100

The calculation has three basic steps:

  1. Subtract the sale price from the original price.
  2. Divide the discount amount by the original price.
  3. Multiply the result by 100.

The original price is the reference amount when calculating the percentage discount. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

Example: How to Calculate Discount Percentage

Suppose a product originally costs $200 and is now selling for $150.

Original Price: $200

Sale Price: $150

Discount = $200 − $150

Discount = $50

Discount Percentage = ($50 ÷ $200) × 100

Discount Percentage = 25%

Therefore, the product has a 25% discount.

How to Calculate the Discount Amount

Sometimes the discount percentage is already given and you need to find the actual amount of money saved.

Discount Amount = Original Price × (Discount Percentage ÷ 100)

For example, suppose a product costs $120 and has a 25% discount.

Discount = $120 × (25 ÷ 100)

Discount = $120 × 0.25

Discount = $30

You save $30 on the purchase.

How to Calculate the Final Sale Price

Once you know the discount amount, subtract it from the original price.

Sale Price = Original Price − Discount Amount

You can also calculate the final price directly:

Sale Price = Original Price × (1 − Discount Percentage ÷ 100)

This direct formula is a standard way to calculate the price after a percentage discount. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

Example: Calculate a Sale Price

A jacket costs $250 and is advertised at 30% off.

Discount = $250 × 30 ÷ 100

Discount = $75

Sale Price = $250 − $75

Sale Price = $175

So the customer saves $75 and pays $175.

How to Calculate the Original Price

You can also work backward when you know the sale price and the discount percentage.

Original Price = Sale Price ÷ (1 − Discount Percentage ÷ 100)

Example

Suppose an item costs $70 after a 30% discount. What was its original price?

Original Price = $70 ÷ (1 − 30 ÷ 100)

Original Price = $70 ÷ 0.70

Original Price = $100

Therefore, the original price was $100.

What Is 10% Off?

A 10% discount means that you save 10% of the original price and pay 90% of the original price.

Sale Price = Original Price × 0.90

For example, 10% off $80 means:

$80 × 0.10 = $8 discount

$80 − $8 = $72 sale price

What Is 20% Off?

A 20% discount means you pay 80% of the original price.

Sale Price = Original Price × 0.80

For example:

$150 × 0.20 = $30 discount

$150 − $30 = $120 sale price

What Is 50% Off?

A 50% discount means the price is reduced by half.

Sale Price = Original Price × 0.50

For example, 50% off $90 means the customer saves $45 and pays $45.

Successive Discounts

Sometimes a product receives more than one discount in sequence. For example, a store may offer 20% off followed by another 10% off.

These discounts should not simply be added together as 30%, because the second discount is applied to the already-reduced price.

Original price = $100

First discount: 20%

$100 − $20 = $80

Second discount: 10% of $80 = $8

Final price = $80 − $8 = $72

The combined reduction is therefore $28, or 28% of the original $100, rather than 30%.

Discount Percentage vs Discount Amount

Discount percentage and discount amount are related but they are not the same thing.

Term Meaning
Original Price Price before the discount
Discount Percentage Percentage reduction from the original price
Discount Amount Actual amount of money saved
Sale Price Price paid after the discount

Common Discount Calculation Mistakes

These mistakes can lead to an incorrect discount percentage or sale price:

  • Dividing the discount amount by the sale price instead of the original price.
  • Forgetting to multiply by 100 when calculating a percentage.
  • Treating a percentage such as 25% as 25 instead of 0.25 when multiplying a price.
  • Adding successive discounts together.
  • Confusing the discount amount with the discount percentage.
  • Forgetting to subtract the discount from the original price.
