How to Calculate a Percentage: Formula, Examples & Easy Methods

Percentages are used in everyday life for shopping discounts, exam scores, financial calculations, statistics, business and many other situations. This guide explains how to calculate a percentage using simple formulas and examples.

What Is a Percentage?

A percentage is a way of expressing a number as a part of 100. The word "percent" means "per hundred." The percentage symbol is %.

For example, 25% means 25 parts out of 100. It can also be written as a fraction or decimal:

  • 25% = 25/100
  • 25% = 0.25
  • 25% = one quarter

Basic Percentage Formula

The most common percentage formula is used when you know the part and the whole.

Percentage = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100

For example, if you scored 45 marks out of 60, you can calculate your percentage as follows:

Example: Exam Score

Part = 45

Whole = 60

Percentage = (45 ÷ 60) × 100

Percentage = 0.75 × 100

Percentage = 75%

How to Calculate a Percentage of a Number

To find a percentage of a number, convert the percentage into a decimal and multiply it by the number.

Percentage of a Number = (Percentage ÷ 100) × Number

Example: 20% of 150

Convert 20% to a decimal:

20 ÷ 100 = 0.20

Multiply by 150:

0.20 × 150 = 30

20% of 150 = 30

How to Find What Percentage One Number Is of Another

Sometimes you want to know what percentage one number represents compared with another number.

Percentage = (First Number ÷ Second Number) × 100

Example: 30 is What Percentage of 120?

Percentage = (30 ÷ 120) × 100

Percentage = 0.25 × 100

Percentage = 25%

How to Calculate Percentage Increase

Percentage increase tells you how much a value has increased compared with its original value.

Percentage Increase = ((New Value − Original Value) ÷ Original Value) × 100

Example: Price Increase

Suppose a product originally costs $80 and its price increases to $100.

Original value = $80

New value = $100

Difference = $100 − $80 = $20

Percentage Increase = ($20 ÷ $80) × 100

Percentage Increase = 25%

How to Calculate Percentage Decrease

Percentage decrease shows how much a value has decreased compared with its original value.

Percentage Decrease = ((Original Value − New Value) ÷ Original Value) × 100

Example: Price Discount

Suppose a product originally costs $100 and the sale price is $75.

Original value = $100

New value = $75

Difference = $100 − $75 = $25

Percentage Decrease = ($25 ÷ $100) × 100

Percentage Decrease = 25%

How to Calculate a Discount Percentage

Percentage calculations are commonly used when shopping. You can calculate the discount percentage by comparing the original price with the sale price.

Discount Percentage = ((Original Price − Sale Price) ÷ Original Price) × 100

Example: 20% Discount

Suppose a product costs $200 and has a 20% discount.

Discount = 20% of $200

Discount = 0.20 × $200

Discount = $40

Sale Price = $200 − $40

Sale Price = $160

Percentage vs Percentage Points

Percentage and percentage points are not always the same thing. Percentage points describe the direct difference between two percentages.

For example, if an interest rate changes from 5% to 7%, the increase is 2 percentage points.

However, the percentage increase relative to the original 5% is:

((7 − 5) ÷ 5) × 100 = 40%

Therefore, the rate increased by 2 percentage points, which represents a 40% increase relative to the original rate.

Common Percentage Calculation Mistakes

Percentage problems are usually straightforward, but a few common mistakes can lead to incorrect results.

  • Using the wrong number as the original or whole value.
  • Forgetting to divide by 100 when converting a percentage to a decimal.
  • Confusing percentage points with percentage change.
  • Using the final value instead of the original value when calculating percentage increase or decrease.
  • Rounding numbers too early during a calculation.
