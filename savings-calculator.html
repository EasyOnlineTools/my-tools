What Is a Savings Calculator?

A savings calculator estimates how much money you could accumulate over time by combining an initial deposit, regular monthly contributions, and interest.

It can help you understand how saving regularly and earning compound interest may affect the growth of your balance over time.

How Savings Growth Is Calculated

The initial deposit grows according to the selected annual interest rate and compounding frequency. Monthly contributions are added at the end of each month and can also earn interest for the remaining months of the savings period.

A = P(1 + r/n)nt

Where P is the initial deposit, r is the annual interest rate as a decimal, n is the number of compounding periods per year, and t is the time in years.

The calculator separately estimates the future value of the regular monthly contributions using an effective monthly rate based on the selected compounding frequency.

Why Regular Contributions Matter

Regular contributions can significantly increase the amount you accumulate over time. Starting earlier and consistently adding money gives your savings more time to potentially earn compound interest.

Savings Calculator FAQs

What does a savings calculator calculate?

It estimates your future savings balance based on your initial deposit, regular monthly contributions, interest rate, compounding frequency, and savings period.

Can I include monthly savings contributions?

Yes. Enter the amount you plan to contribute each month. The calculator adds these contributions at the end of each month and estimates their growth over the remaining savings period.

What is compound interest?

Compound interest is interest earned on the original amount plus previously accumulated interest.

Which compounding frequency should I select?

Select the frequency that most closely matches the account or savings product you are evaluating. Common frequencies include monthly, quarterly, semi-annually, and annually.

What happens if the interest rate is 0%?

When the interest rate is 0%, no interest is added. The final balance is simply the initial deposit plus the monthly contributions.
