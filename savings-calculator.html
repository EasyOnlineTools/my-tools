What Is a Savings Calculator?

A savings calculator estimates how much money you could accumulate over time by combining an initial deposit, regular contributions, and interest.

It can help you understand how regular saving and compound interest can affect the growth of your savings.

How Savings Growth Is Calculated

The calculator compounds the initial deposit according to the selected interest rate and compounding frequency. Regular monthly contributions are also added throughout the savings period.

A = P(1 + r/n)nt

Where P is the initial deposit, r is the annual interest rate, n is the number of compounding periods per year, and t is the time in years.

Example Savings Calculation

Suppose you start with $5,000, contribute $200 each month, earn an annual interest rate of 5%, and save for 10 years.

The calculator estimates your final balance by combining your deposits and the interest earned during the savings period.

Why Regular Contributions Matter

Regular contributions can significantly increase the amount you accumulate over time. The longer your money remains invested or saved, the more opportunity there is for compound interest to increase the balance.

Savings Calculator FAQs

What does a savings calculator calculate?

It estimates your future savings balance based on your initial deposit, regular contributions, interest rate, compounding frequency, and savings period.

Can I include monthly savings contributions?

Yes. Enter the amount you plan to contribute each month and the calculator will include those contributions in the estimated final balance.

What is compound interest?

Compound interest is interest earned on both the original amount and previously accumulated interest.

Which compounding frequency should I select?

Select the frequency that most closely matches the account or savings product you are evaluating. Common frequencies include monthly, quarterly, and annually.
