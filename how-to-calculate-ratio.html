How to Calculate Ratio: Formula, Examples & Easy Guide

Ratios are used to compare quantities in mathematics, cooking, business, finance, science, construction and everyday life. Learning how to calculate a ratio makes it easier to compare two or more quantities and understand their relationship.

In this guide, you will learn how to calculate a ratio, simplify ratios, find equivalent ratios, compare quantities and solve common ratio problems with easy examples.

What Is a Ratio?

A ratio is a mathematical way of comparing two or more quantities. It shows how much of one quantity there is compared with another.

A ratio can be written using a colon, as a fraction, or using words.

Example:

2:3
2 to 3
2/3

These forms represent the same comparison when used in the appropriate context.

How to Calculate a Ratio

To calculate a ratio between two quantities, follow these simple steps:

  1. Identify the two quantities you want to compare.
  2. Write them in the correct order.
  3. Separate the quantities with a colon.
  4. Simplify the ratio if possible.
Ratio = First Quantity : Second Quantity

Example: Calculate a Ratio

Suppose a classroom has 12 boys and 18 girls. The ratio of boys to girls is:

Boys : Girls

12 : 18

Divide both numbers by 6:

12 ÷ 6 : 18 ÷ 6

2 : 3

Therefore, the simplified ratio of boys to girls is 2:3.

How to Simplify a Ratio

A ratio can be simplified by dividing all of its parts by the same common factor.

The greatest common factor, or GCF, is often used to simplify a ratio completely.

Example: Simplify 20:30

Ratio = 20:30

The greatest common factor of 20 and 30 is 10.

20 ÷ 10 : 30 ÷ 10

2:3

Ratio Formula

A basic ratio compares two quantities:

Ratio = A : B

Here, A represents the first quantity and B represents the second quantity.

The order matters. For example, 2:3 is not the same ratio as 3:2.

How to Find a Ratio From Two Numbers

Suppose you want to find the ratio between 15 and 25.

Step 1: Write the numbers in order.

15:25

Step 2: Find the greatest common factor.

GCF = 5

Step 3: Divide both numbers by 5.

15 ÷ 5 : 25 ÷ 5

3:5

How to Calculate a Ratio With Three Numbers

Ratios can compare more than two quantities.

For example, suppose a basket contains:

4 apples, 6 oranges and 8 bananas.

The ratio is:

4:6:8

The greatest common factor is 2.

4 ÷ 2 : 6 ÷ 2 : 8 ÷ 2

2:3:4

Equivalent Ratios

Equivalent ratios have the same relationship between their quantities.

You can create an equivalent ratio by multiplying or dividing every part of the ratio by the same non-zero number.

Starting ratio: 2:3

Multiply both parts by 2:

4:6

Multiply both parts by 3:

6:9

Therefore:

2:3 = 4:6 = 6:9

How to Find a Missing Number in a Ratio

Ratios can also be used to find an unknown quantity.

Suppose:

2:3 = 8:x

Since 2 becomes 8 by multiplying by 4, multiply 3 by 4:

3 × 4 = 12

Therefore:

x = 12

Ratios and Fractions

A ratio can often be written as a fraction.

For example:

3:5 = 3/5

However, the meaning depends on the context. A ratio compares quantities, while a fraction can represent a part of a whole.

Ratios and Percentages

Ratios can also be converted into percentages when the quantities represent parts of a whole.

For example, suppose the ratio of red balls to blue balls is 2:3. The total number of parts is:

2 + 3 = 5

Red share:

2 ÷ 5 × 100 = 40%

Blue share:

3 ÷ 5 × 100 = 60%

Ratio Example in Cooking

Ratios are commonly used in recipes.

Suppose a recipe requires flour and sugar in a ratio of 3:1.

This means that for every 3 parts of flour, you need 1 part of sugar.

Flour : Sugar = 3:1

If you use 6 cups of flour, multiply both parts by 2:

6:2

Therefore, you need 2 cups of sugar.

Ratio Example With Money

Suppose two people divide $500 in the ratio 2:3.

First, add the ratio parts:

2 + 3 = 5

Value of one part:

$500 ÷ 5 = $100

First share:

2 × $100 = $200

Second share:

3 × $100 = $300

Ratio Example in a Classroom

Suppose a classroom has 20 students, including 8 boys and 12 girls.

The ratio of boys to girls is:

8:12

Divide both numbers by 4:

2:3

The ratio of boys to girls is therefore 2:3.

Part-to-Part vs Part-to-Whole Ratios

It is important to understand what two quantities are being compared.

Type Example Meaning
Part-to-Part 2:3 Compares one part with another part
Part-to-Whole 2:5 Compares one part with the total

How to Compare Two Ratios

One way to compare ratios is to convert them into fractions or use cross multiplication.

Consider:

2:3 and 3:5

Compare:

2/3 and 3/5

Cross multiply:

2 × 5 = 10

3 × 3 = 9

Since 10 is greater than 9:

2:3 is greater than 3:5

Common Mistakes When Calculating Ratios

  • Reversing the order of the quantities.
  • Dividing only one part of the ratio.
  • Using different factors for different parts.
  • Forgetting to simplify the ratio.
  • Confusing a part-to-part ratio with a part-to-whole ratio.
  • Ignoring the units when quantities are measured differently.

Ratios With Different Units

Before comparing two quantities using a ratio, make sure their units are compatible.

For example, if you want to compare 1 meter with 50 centimeters, convert them to the same unit first.

1 meter = 100 centimeters

Ratio:

100:50

Simplify:

2:1
