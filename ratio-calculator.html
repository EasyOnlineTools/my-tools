    What Is a Ratio?

    A ratio compares two quantities and shows the relationship between them. It is commonly written using a colon, such as 2:3. The first number represents one quantity and the second number represents the other.

    For example, if a basket contains 10 apples and 15 oranges, the ratio of apples to oranges is 10:15. Dividing both numbers by 5 gives the simplified ratio 2:3.

    How to Calculate Ratio: Formula, Examples & Easy Guide

    How to Simplify a Ratio

    To simplify a ratio, divide both numbers by their greatest common factor (GCF). The greatest common factor is the largest positive whole number that divides both numbers without a remainder.

    Example: Simplify 12:18

    Step 1: Find the greatest common factor of 12 and 18.

    Step 2: The GCF is 6.

    Step 3: Divide both numbers by 6.

    12 ÷ 6 : 18 ÷ 6 = 2:3

    Ratio Formula

    A ratio is written in the form A:B, where A and B represent two quantities being compared. To simplify a ratio of whole numbers, divide both parts by their greatest common factor.

    Simplified ratio = (A ÷ GCF) : (B ÷ GCF)

    Equivalent Ratios

    Equivalent ratios represent the same relationship between two quantities. You can create an equivalent ratio by multiplying or dividing both parts by the same non-zero number.

    Examples of equivalent ratios:

    2:3

    4:6

    6:9

    8:12

    All these ratios represent the same relationship.

    Ratios with Decimal Numbers

    Ratios can also contain decimal values. For example, the ratio 2.5:5 can be converted into 25:50 by multiplying both numbers by 10. Dividing both numbers by 25 gives the simplified ratio 1:2.

    Ratio, Decimal and Percentage

    When the second number is greater than zero, the relationship between two quantities can also be expressed as a decimal or percentage. Divide the first number by the second number to calculate the decimal comparison. Multiply the result by 100 to express it as a percentage.

    Example: 2:5

    Decimal comparison: 2 ÷ 5 = 0.4

    Percentage comparison: 0.4 × 100 = 40%

    Common Uses of Ratios

    Ratios are useful in mathematics, cooking, recipes, maps, scale drawings, measurements, business, statistics and everyday comparisons. They help explain how one quantity relates to another.

    Ratio Calculator FAQs

    What does a ratio such as 2:3 mean?

    A ratio of 2:3 means that for every 2 units of the first quantity, there are 3 units of the second quantity.

    How do I simplify a ratio?

    Divide both numbers by their greatest common factor. For example, 12:18 becomes 2:3 after dividing both numbers by 6.

    Can this calculator simplify decimal ratios?

    Yes. You can enter decimal numbers with up to 10 decimal places. The calculator converts them into exact whole-number values before simplifying the ratio.

    Can I enter zero in a ratio?

    Yes. One number can be zero, but both numbers cannot be zero. When the second number is zero, the decimal and percentage comparisons are not available because division by zero is undefined.

    Can I enter negative numbers?

    This calculator is designed for non-negative quantities, so negative values are not accepted.

    What are equivalent ratios?

    Equivalent ratios are different ratios that describe the same relationship. For example, 2:3, 4:6 and 6:9 are equivalent.

    What is the difference between a ratio and a fraction?

    A ratio compares two quantities, while a fraction represents part of a whole or a division. Both can be written using two numbers, but their meanings depend on the context.
